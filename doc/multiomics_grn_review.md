# Multi-omics integration for GRN inference: challenges and recent work

*Literature review, October 2026. 119 papers (2006–2026; 51 from 2025–2026; 14 preprints). Full table: `multiomics_grn_literature_table.csv`.*

## Summary

So far, adding data layers to gene regulatory network (GRN) inference has mostly narrowed the set of candidate edges; it has added little independent evidence. The 2024–2026 benchmarks of single-cell multiome methods find that networks are driven almost entirely by RNA. Scrambling the ATAC signal or breaking RNA–ATAC cell pairing barely changes accuracy, while scrambling expression collapses it ([Karamveer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77838-w)). Expression-only algorithms beat multimodal pipelines ([Nourisa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.25.640181), preprint). Methods agree on regulators and targets but almost never on edges, with a median edge overlap of 0.021 ([Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.20.629764), preprint). Against knockout references, most methods score close to random.

Knowledge-driven integration (the CARNIVAL → COSMOS → CORNETO line) does not need large sample sizes, but has its own structural limits. It can only find mechanisms that are already in the prior-knowledge network (PKN). Its results follow node degree and the choice of resource. It returns many equally optimal subnetworks. And it encodes metabolites with sign rules that cannot represent mass balance.

Recent work responds in five ways: (i) benchmarks anchored on perturbation data and maintained as living benchmarks; (ii) transfer of external evidence (bulk atlases, CRISPR-tested enhancer–gene pairs, ChIP atlases, sequence-model priors) into single-cell inference; (iii) dynamical models that couple regulation to kinetics; (iv) foundation models, which so far do not beat simple baselines; and (v) mechanistic optimisation fitted across many samples or in factor space.

## Families of integration methods

Methods differ in what the non-RNA layer contributes and in what an edge means. The distinction matters because the challenges below hit each family differently.

| Family                                   | Representative methods                                                                            | Role of the non-RNA layer                                                                                | Edge semantics                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Enhancer-driven single-cell GRNs         | SCENIC+, CellOracle, Pando, FigR, GRaNIE, LINGER, Dictys, DIRECT-NET, TRIPOD, scMTNI, scMEGA      | ATAC peaks plus motifs define candidate TF→CRE→gene triplets; weights are mostly fitted on expression    | Unsigned or signed TF→target, cell-type or state level |
| Prior-knowledge causal contextualisation | decoupleR + CollecTRI/DoRothEA, CARNIVAL, COSMOS/COSMOS+, PHONEMeS, CORNETO, SIGMA                | Footprint-derived activities (TF, kinase) and metabolite measurements become constraints on a signed PKN | Signed, directed paths consistent with data            |
| Statistical and factor-based multi-omics | MOFA/MOFA+, MEFISTO, SOFA, KiMONo, MORE, Lemonite, MINIE, GENIE3/ARACNe as single-layer baselines | Layers enter as co-varying features or regressors                                                        | Associations or latent axes, mostly unsigned           |
| Deep and foundation models               | scGPT, Geneformer, scPRINT, GET, scTFBridge, HyperG-VAE                                           | Pretraining on atlases; edges read from attention, embeddings or attributions                            | Post hoc scores, rarely validated causally             |

The most cited reviews frame the field this way. They cover the single-cell multi-omic era ([Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-023-00618-5); [Loers and Vermeirssen 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbae382)), knowledge-based signalling models ([Garrido-Rodriguez et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.202211036); [Svinin & Glaab 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaf654)), the use of priors ([Stock et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44320-025-00088-3)), and the shift from correlative to causal models ([Maizels and Briscoe 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-026-00939-1); [Tejada-Lapuerta et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02124-2)).

## Challenges in evaluation

### No ground truth exists

Every substrate used for evaluation captures a different notion of regulation, and the choice of reference changes method rankings more than the choice of dataset does. ChIP-seq measures binding, not function. Knockouts measure function but mix direct and indirect effects. Across 11 methods and 7 datasets, rankings varied more between ChIP-based and perturbation-based gold standards than between datasets ([McCalla et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/g3journal/jkad004)). BEAR-GRN built four reference definitions (ChIP, knockout, union, intersection). ChIP and knockout references share about half of their target genes but only a small fraction of edges, and the rankings of SCENIC+ and GRaNIE flip between them ([Karamveer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77838-w)).

Curated resources also disagree with one another. For shared TFs, the mean Jaccard index between CollecTRI and the other regulon collections is about 0.01 ([Müller-Dott et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad841)). Curated, ChIP, motif and co-expression regulons overlap little ([Garcia-Alonso et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.240663.118)). The same holds for signalling. In the EGF benchmark, up to 90% of inferred interactions were absent from every ground-truth set, so a missing edge cannot be classed as either a false positive or a discovery ([Garrido-Rodriguez et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69332-0)). When the same databases serve as both prior and reference, recovery scores become circular ([Stock et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44320-025-00088-3)).

### Accuracy is close to baselines

Performance against reference networks has been modest for a decade. DREAM5 showed that recovery in eukaryotes is far below that in prokaryotes, and that a community consensus network was the most robust ([Marbach et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2016)). In BEELINE, 11 of 12 single-cell algorithms had median early precision near or below random on at least one curated model ([Pratapa et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0690-6)). Other early single-cell benchmarks reached the same conclusion ([Chen and Mar 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-018-2217-z)). Multiome methods have not changed this. Most per-dataset AUROCs in BEAR-GRN lie roughly between 0.4 and 0.65, and only TRIPOD was consistently above random against knockout and intersection references ([Karamveer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77838-w); these values were read from figure data and are approximate).

The same pattern holds for prediction tasks. Network structures from motifs, ChIP or co-expression rarely improved forecasts of held-out perturbations over empty-network or mean baselines ([Kernfeld et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03840-y)). None of five foundation models, nor GEARS or CPA, beat an additive baseline for double perturbations ([Ahlmann-Eltze et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6)). Similar results were reported by [Csendes et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-025-11600-2), [Wong et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf317) and [Wei et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02980-0). Part of the apparent skill in perturbation prediction comes from systematic differences between perturbed and control cells, not from perturbation-specific signal ([Viñas Torné et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-025-02777-8)). A counterpoint argues that common metrics such as MSE and control-referenced Pearson delta are poorly calibrated, and that deep models do beat uninformative baselines once the metrics are calibrated ([Miller et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.20.683304), preprint). The question is not settled. Both baselines and metric calibration are now considered necessary.

### Simulators and benchmark errors

Simulators give exact ground truth, but their conclusions often fail to transfer to real data. Methods did better on BoolODE synthetic networks than on curated models ([Pratapa et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0690-6)). Interventional methods win on synthetic data but not on Perturb-seq ([Chevalley et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07764-y)). GRouNdGAN was built because simulated and real benchmark results disagree ([Zinati et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48516-6)). Multi-omic methods outperform expression-only methods on scMultiSim, where accessibility gates expression by construction ([Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02651-0)), but not on real data. Simulation now covers bulk multilayer designs ([Monzó et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaf110)), but none of the simulators found here model metabolite or lipid layers.

The benchmarks themselves can contain errors. A re-analysis found that CellOracle's ChIP-based comparison with other GRN methods was implemented incorrectly ([Nourisa et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.10.16.618746), preprint). Edge-level metrics also miss the topological properties, such as hubs, that downstream analyses rely on ([Stock et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae267)).

## Challenges in integration

### The extra modality adds little

The most important finding of 2024–2026 is negative. In BEAR-GRN's modality-ablation experiments, only LINGER and TRIPOD were sensitive to RNA–ATAC pairing, and the authors describe current multiome methods as predominantly RNA-driven ([Karamveer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77838-w)). Networks inferred from paired and from computationally paired data share only about a third of their edges ([Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.20.629764)). The architecture explains this. In CellOracle, Pando and SCENIC+, accessibility mainly filters the candidate edges through motif scanning, and edge weights are then fitted on expression ([Kamimoto et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05688-9); [Fleck et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05279-8); [Bravo González-Blas et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01938-4)). Integration does help when external data carry independent information, as when LINGER pretrains on atlas-scale bulk data ([Yuan & Duren 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02182-7)). The benefit therefore comes from new evidence, not from a second readout of the same cells.

### Linking enhancers to genes and TFs

Each step from CRE to gene and from TF to CRE has an accuracy problem of its own. On 9,225 CRISPR-tested enhancer–gene pairs in K562, only supervised scE2G models and ABC-based models beat genomic-distance baselines. The widely used correlation- and regression-based linkers did not ([Sheth et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-026-02695-8)). Accessibility at domains of regulatory chromatin precedes expression ([Ma et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.056)), and the lag between the two layers can be measured ([Li et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-022-01476-y)). This lag weakens same-cell correlation as evidence for a link. Newer linkers model counts directly, for example with tile-level regression ([Mitra et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01689-8)), latent-variable models with calibrated p-values ([Su et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59306-z)) or Hi-C priors ([Liang et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.09.681330), preprint).

On the TF side, paralogous TFs share near-identical motifs. Motif- and footprint-based evidence cannot tell them apart ([Yuan & Duren 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02182-7)), chromVAR assigns all family members the same score ([Schep et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4401)), and anticorrelated family members produce spurious repressor calls in SCENIC+ ([Bravo González-Blas et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01938-4)). ChIP-defined binding sites ([Włodarczyk et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62252-5)) and sequence-model footprints ([Hu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08443-4)) reduce the problem but do not resolve co-expressed paralogs.

### TF activity is not TF mRNA

Most multiome GRNs use TF transcript levels as the regulator variable, but TF activity is also set by ligands, phosphorylation, cofactors and degradation. Epiregulon was built for this gap. It scores activity from co-occurrence of expression and accessibility at ChIP-defined sites, and it predicted responses to an AR antagonist, an AR degrader and a SMARCA4 degrader ([Włodarczyk et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62252-5)). Footprint-based activity inference from target genes avoids the problem by design. Its accuracy, however, depends more on the regulon resource than on the statistic ([Holland et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-020-1949-z); [Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioadv/vbac016); [Zhu et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag513)). It also favours TFs with many curated targets.

### Unpaired data and data sparsity

Many datasets profile RNA and chromatin in different cells, and the computational cell pairing used to bridge them introduces error. In an unpaired-integration benchmark, gene-activity scores correlated only weakly with measured expression, even though cell neighbourhoods were preserved. Dimension reduction was the most important step, and optimal-transport label transfer performed best ([Naqing et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04071-5)). Approaches used in practice include cell matching in FigR ([Kartha et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2022.100166)), guidance-graph embeddings in scGLUE ([Cao & Gao 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-022-01284-4)) and pseudo-multiome averaging in SCENIC+.

Single-cell ATAC is close to binary per peak, so methods aggregate cells into metacells, pseudobulks or trajectories, and the result depends on the choice. BEAR-GRN found LINGER and DIRECT-NET stable under subsampling, while FigR and SCENIC+ varied more. Under a fixed budget (16 cores, 256 GB, 48 h), STREAM did not finish ([Karamveer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77838-w)). Measurement technology has moved past RNA+ATAC, with multi-mark CUT&Tag ([Bartošovič & Castelo-Branco 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-022-01535-4)), joint methylation, accessibility and RNA ([Argelaguet et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1825-8)) and single-cell Hi-C ([Chang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02447-1)). Few GRN methods use these layers. This assessment rests on keyword searches and is provisional.

## Challenges in causality and priors

### Causality, direction and identifiability

Observational co-variation cannot separate direct from indirect effects, and in most multiome methods the direction TF → CRE → gene is imposed by the motif prior rather than learned. Multimodal GRNs predict held-out expression moderately well but fail to recover causal responses in perturbation assays ([Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.20.629764)). SCENIC+ is biased towards activating edges ([Bravo González-Blas et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01938-4)). Interventional data have not yet delivered the expected gains. On CausalBench (more than 200,000 interventional cells), methods that used the interventions did not beat observational methods ([Chevalley et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07764-y)). Interventional structure learning recovers GRNs only with strong interventions, large samples and low noise ([Sprengel and Velten 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.05.692565), preprint).

Knowledge-driven methods are not identifiable in a different way: sign-consistency integer linear programs (ILPs) usually have many optimal or near-optimal subnetworks. In CORNETO, single-sample fits of similar samples could share no edges at all. A multi-sample structured-sparsity penalty gave smaller and more consistent networks, but it assumes shared structure and needs a tuned λ. For multi-sample iMAT at λ = 1.5, precision rose by 29.6% and recall fell by 8.6% ([Rodriguez-Mier et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01069-9)). SIGMA enumerates alternative receptor-to-TF routes to make this ambiguity explicit ([Liaskos et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.19.733348), preprint).

### Instability and missing uncertainty

Any single inferred network is one draw from a broad distribution. Multiome GRNs change with the random seed and when a single pipeline step is swapped, and Dictys, SCENIC+ and SCENIC had seed-to-seed edge overlap around or below 0.5 ([Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.20.629764)). Reproducibility between independent datasets of the same condition is limited; GENIE3 was the most reproducible method ([Kang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2021.617282)). Few methods report edge-level uncertainty. PMF-GRN was introduced for this reason ([Skok Gibbs et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03226-6)). For other methods, confidence has to be obtained after the fact by bootstrapping or stability selection.

### Bias, coverage and context of priors

Prior knowledge improves inference, but it also bounds and biases it. Prior knowledge combined with TF-activity estimation gave the best single-cell performance in [McCalla et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/g3journal/jkad004), and the quality of the prior largely determines what GRN inference can achieve ([Skok Gibbs et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77381-8)). Three problems follow.

1. Degree bias. Active-module methods produced no more meaningful modules on real networks than on degree-preserving randomised ones ([Lazareva et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbab066)). In compound mechanism-of-action benchmarks, target recovery correlated with PKN degree (mean Spearman ρ 0.38–0.56 for some algorithms on OmniPath), and the combination of algorithm and network determined performance ([Hosseini-Gerami et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-023-05277-1)).
2. Resource dependence. For kinase activity, the choice of library mattered more than the algorithm ([Müller-Dott et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59779-y)). TF regulon collections also overlap little (see above).
3. Context. OmniPath integrates more than 100 resources but is largely context-agnostic ([Türei et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20209923); [Türei et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf1126)). Context therefore enters only through the data. Weighting literature edges by disease or cell context improved agreement with transcriptomics ([Tsutsui et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag101)). General-purpose LLMs are weak sources of priors, reaching roughly 48–50% balanced accuracy on TF–target queries against TRRUST ([Noel et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf653)).

### Metabolites and lipids in causal networks

Metabolomics and lipidomics are the least developed layers. COSMOS converts metabolic reactions into signed edges in which a substrate activates its enzyme and the enzyme activates its product. Its authors state that this assumes metabolite levels depend only on production and not on consumption ([Dugourd et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20209730)). Substrate accumulation behind a blocked enzyme therefore cannot be represented with the correct sign. Cofactors and promiscuous metabolites have to be removed so that they do not short-circuit the network, and part of metabolism is lost with them.

Metabolite-to-protein regulatory edges are also sparse. The protein–metabolite interactome lags far behind protein–protein and protein–DNA knowledge ([Piazza et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.12.006)). MetalinksDB extends coverage substantially, but many of its edges lack sign or direction ([Farr et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbae347)). Without such edges, metabolites mostly appear as downstream readouts rather than as regulators of TFs. For lipidomics, enzyme multispecificity makes the mapping from lipid species to enzymes ambiguous ([Rose et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbac572)). CORNETO can add flux constraints through hypergraph and FBA formulations, but combining these with signed signalling remains a modelling choice ([Rodriguez-Mier et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01069-9)).

### Sample size, context and dynamics

Data-driven inference needs many diverse samples, and fitting a network per cell type or condition divides the sample further. GENIE3 and ARACNe estimates are unreliable at small n ([Huynh-Thu et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0012776); [Margolin et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-s1-s7)). Recent multi-omic regulatory methods are demonstrated on cohorts of 75–200 or on simulations ([Vandemoortele et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.27.714373), preprint; [Aguerralde-Martin et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaf270)). Multi-task learning along lineages trades context specificity against power ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38637-9)).

Static, population-level networks also miss rewiring over time, between cells and across space ([Wang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01971-3); [Zhang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag324)). Comparing networks between conditions is harder still. Differential methods validate their real-data findings mainly through enrichment and literature ([Galindez et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioadv/vbae034); [Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf352)), and different network types (signalling, TF, co-expression) carry different edge semantics ([Unger Avila et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41581-024-00849-7)).

## Recent work, 2024–2026

### Living, perturbation-anchored benchmarks

Benchmarking has become a research area of its own. GRETA recombines individual pipeline steps across methods ([Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.20.629764)). BEAR-GRN combines several ground-truth definitions with stability, scalability and modality-ablation tests ([Karamveer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77838-w)). geneRNIB is a continuously updated benchmark that scores networks on perturbation data rather than on fixed references ([Nourisa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.25.640181)). Genome-scale Perturb-seq ([Replogle et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2022.05.013)) made interventional evaluation possible through CausalBench and expression-forecasting benchmarks ([Chevalley et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07764-y); [Kernfeld et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03840-y)). Signalling has its counterparts in NetworkCommons and the EGF pathway benchmark ([Paton et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf048); [Garrido-Rodriguez et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69332-0)).

### Transfer of external evidence

To compensate for weak single-cell signal, methods import independent experimental evidence. LINGER pretrains on bulk atlases ([Yuan & Duren 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02182-7)). scE2G trains enhancer–gene classifiers on CRISPR-tested pairs and evaluates them by GWAS and eQTL enrichment ([Sheth et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-026-02695-8)). Epiregulon uses ChIP binding-site atlases ([Włodarczyk et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62252-5)), and GLM-Prior derives priors from genomic language models that transfer across species ([Skok Gibbs et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77381-8)). This is the clearest current route by which integration adds information rather than constraints.

### Dynamical and spatial GRNs

Several methods couple regulation to models of time. Dictys uses stochastic process networks ([Wang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01971-3)), Velorama applies Granger causality on velocity-derived cell graphs ([Singh et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2024.04.005)), and RegVelo learns splicing kinetics jointly with a GRN. RegVelo's in silico perturbations were validated by CRISPR knockout and Perturb-seq ([Wang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.04.022)). Spatial and cell-specific GRNs are appearing ([Hu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.21.671434), preprint; [Zhang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag324); [Bravo González-Blas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-023-01316-4)). Each of these methods addresses one axis (time, cell or space), and none yet combines it with multi-omic causal inference.

### Foundation models and their critiques

Pretrained models are being used as GRN engines. Examples are Geneformer ([Theodoris et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06139-9)), scGPT ([Cui et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02201-0)), scPRINT, which was pretrained on 50 million cells for network prediction ([Kalfon et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-58699-1)), and GET, which was trained on chromatin accessibility and sequence across cell types ([Fu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08391-z)). The critiques are consistent. Zero-shot embeddings are often beaten by simpler methods ([Kedzierska et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03574-x)). Attention-derived edges added no value for predicting perturbation targets over gene-level baselines (AUROC about 0.70 vs 0.81–0.88), and ablating putative regulatory heads did not degrade predictions ([Kendiukhov 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-026-12965-8)). A preprint reports that zero-shot scGPT token embeddings do beat classical baselines on STRING and ChIP references, so the outcome depends on how edges are extracted ([Ye et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.17.725083)).

### Multi-sample and factor-space mechanisms

The knowledge-driven line is being unified and extended. CORNETO recasts CARNIVAL, iMAT, prize-collecting Steiner trees and FBA as network-flow problems fitted jointly across samples ([Rodriguez-Mier et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01069-9)). COSMOS+ derives TF, kinase and ligand–receptor activities from MOFA factor weights and propagates them through the COSMOS PKN ([Dugourd et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.07.15.603538), preprint). SOFA makes factor analysis semi-supervised, so that known covariates no longer dominate the factors ([Capraz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74694-6)). Factor models are thus becoming an upstream step for PKN reasoning rather than an end point. Other methods target identifiability and balance: SIGMA enforces elemental balance and enumerates alternative routes ([Liaskos et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.19.733348), preprint), and MINIE models the separation of timescales between metabolite and transcript layers ([Moscardó García et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-025-00591-1)).

### Metabolite knowledge and regulators

Two developments target the metabolite gap. Knowledge resources are growing: MetalinksDB ([Farr et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbae347)) and the 2025 OmniPath update, which adds enzyme–PTM and intercellular layers ([Türei et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf1126)). Data-driven module networks, in turn, treat metabolites as regulators of gene programmes ([Vandemoortele et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.27.714373), preprint). Hypergraph and regression models of multi-omic regulation continue to appear ([Su et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2025.101026); [Aguerralde-Martin et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaf270)). They give statistical rather than signed causal edges and are mostly validated on simulations.

## Implications for a CORNETO evaluation

The literature maps directly onto an evaluation of CORNETO on bulk RNA-seq, metabolomics and lipidomics with a 3-versus-3 design.

1. No benchmark with ground truth was found for CARNIVAL-, COSMOS- or CORNETO-type contextualisation of transcriptomics plus metabolomics, and no simulator models metabolite or lipid layers. A planted-route simulation tied to the real PKN would fill a gap the literature names but has not closed.
2. Robustness should be reported as distributions, not as a single network: edge frequencies across alternative optima, across sparsity penalties (λ) and across regulon resources (CollecTRI vs DoRothEA). The evidence that the resource can matter more than the algorithm makes the regulon choice an experimental factor ([Müller-Dott et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad841); [Müller-Dott et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59779-y)).
3. Degree-preserving rewired PKNs and label permutations are the negative controls the field now expects, given the documented degree bias ([Lazareva et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbab066); [Hosseini-Gerami et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-023-05277-1)).
4. With three samples per group, GENIE3 and MOFA+ comparators will be underpowered, which is itself a finding to report. Multi-sample pooling is the main argument for CORNETO here, but its shared-structure assumption should be tested rather than assumed.
5. The sign rules of COSMOS-style metabolite edges cannot represent substrate accumulation (for example upstream of HMGCR). Any route recovered through those edges should be checked against mass balance outside the PKN.
6. Whether a route proposed in the literature is recoverable is a test of PKN coverage. A route that the ILP finds only when it is supplied as input, or that is absent from OmniPath, is evidence about the PKN, not about the biology ([Dugourd et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20209730)).

## Open gaps

The literature leaves six gaps open. Each is anchored to the papers above.

1. There is no agreed functional ground truth for multi-omic regulatory edges; ChIP-seq, knockout and curated references disagree at the edge level.
2. It is unclear whether non-RNA modalities add independent evidence inside current GRN methods; on real data, ablation experiments say they mostly do not.
3. Signed causal integration lacks a representation of metabolite consumption and mass balance, and it lacks signed metabolite → TF edges.
4. Edge-level uncertainty and the many equally optimal solutions of ILP methods are rarely reported.
5. Perturbation data have not yet produced better causal GRNs on real screens, and no multiome perturbation benchmark yet covers metabolic perturbations.
6. Most methods address time, space or modality separately; combinations remain rare, and validation of differential (disease vs control) GRNs relies on enrichment.
